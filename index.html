Brief Introduction to Integrability and Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz

2025-12-12
projects
course, integrable-systems

We present an introduction to quantum integrability through the example of the 1D interacting Bose gas, known as the Lieb–Liniger model. Starting from the two-body scattering problem, we derive the exact S-matrix and illustrate how factorized scattering leads to the Yang–Baxter equation and an infinite set of conserved charges. These structures provide the microscopic origin of integrability in one dimension. We then introduce the thermodynamic Bethe ansatz (TBA) formalism and derive the integral equation governing the pseudo-energy. Finally, we solve the TBA equation numerically and analyze the resulting momentum and energy distributions at finite temperature. The numerical results demonstrate the qualitative effects of interactions and are consistent with physical expectations for 1D quantum systems.

The Database of Maxwellian Averaged Neutron Capture Cross Section (MACS) of the CERN Neutron Time-of-flight Facility (n_TOF) for Nuclear Astrophysics

2025-12-10
projects
astrophysics, database, internship

We construct a database for all available isotopes’ Maxwellian-Averaged Cross-Sections (MACS) measured at the CERN n_TOF facility. In addition to the experimental data, the database contains theoretical values calculated by the TALYS-2.0 program, as well as evaluated values from other libraries, for reference. Quantities such as Q-value, reaction rate, and stellar enhancement factor (SEF) are included for completeness. Our programming enables quick updates and modifications to data, with the entire process automated. It requires minimal input and provides a high degree of freedom to add comments or change the plotting style. The database contains complete information ready for theoretical simulation of nuclear-astrophysical processes, and MACS measurements at the CERN n_TOF facility are, to our knowledge, the newest and of high resolution.

Brief Introduction to Effective Theory of Binary Inspirals

2025-07-12
projects
EFT, course

My EFT course report is a brief introduction to the effective theory of binary inspirals. The attached file is the manuscript of my blackboard presentation. First, I introduced the setup, including the hierarchy, the power counting parameter, and the action. I categorized the modes of the gravitational field by their momenta, which is crucial for accurately calculating the effective action, including both the potential and the radiation. Next, I calculate the effective potential term for binary inspirals, emphasizing the use of Feynman diagrams and power counting. This approach allows us to reproduce the post-Newtonian potential terms, term by term, within this EFT formalism.

Brief Introduction to Conformal Bootstrap

2025-07-12
projects
bootstrap, course

My QFT II course report is a brief introduction to the conformal bootstrap. The initial chapters introduce the fundamental concepts of conformal field theory. We introduce the conformal group using embedding formalism, which provides a clear perspective on quantization. Next, we derive the OPE associativity in Euclidean signature, which serves as a consistent condition in the conformal bootstrap for higher-point correlation functions. Finally, we present some helpful coordinates for the four-point correlator and outline the numerical conformal bootstrap algorithm used to determine the spectrum of consistent theories.
