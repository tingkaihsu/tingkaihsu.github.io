These are some thoughts from reading the lecture notes written by J. Kaplan. I currently don’t have a good understanding of quantum gravity, and what I know comes mainly from fragmentary information: the effective field theory of quantum gravity. The field-theory approach had originally been neglected because of the renormalization problem, but in recent years it has received renewed attention in this field because of its usefulness in approximately describing gravitational waves. I personally think this direction leans more toward phenomenology. I also believe that the most central understanding must come from a quantum description of black holes.

Euclidean Version and the Poincare Patch

By analytic continuation $t\to it$, the embedding space of the $AdS_{d+1}$ spacetime becomes

$$ X_0^2 - \sum_{i}^{d+1}X_i^2 = R^2\,, \tag{1} $$where $R$ is the AdS radius. The Poincare Patch coordinate is$$ X_0 = R \frac{\cosh t}{\cos \rho} \equiv \frac12 \left(\frac{z^2+\vec r^2+R^2}{z}\right)\,,\\ X_{d+1} = R\frac{\sinh t}{\cos \rho} \equiv \frac12 \left(\frac{z^2+\vec r^2-R^2}{z}\right)\,,\\ X_i = \Omega_i R\tan \rho \equiv \frac Rz r_i. $$where $\vec r$ is the spacial d vector. Now, I have a question about Kaplan’s argument that $z$ should run from $0$ to $\infty$, and he argues that this is because $X_0$ has a fixed sign such that Eq.(1) holds even $\sum X_i^2 = 0$. I think the most obvious way to say that $X_0$ is positive is from the second parameterization $R\frac{\cosh t}{\cos \rho}$ for $t\in (-\infty, \infty)$ and $\rho \in [0, \pi/2)$.
