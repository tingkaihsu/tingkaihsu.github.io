Ting-Kai Hsu
2026-10-23

Introduction

This is the second research project I have worked on. What draws me to it is that the formalism is remarkably simple, yet the results leave a great deal of room for exploration. The physical assumptions it relies on are very basic, and the results can reproduce and check the consistency of existing theories, and also let us probe the physics at the boundary of the allowed space, where the bounds are saturated. To me, it resembles a phase diagram in thermodynamics: a single plot in which one can keep finding interesting regions to examine.

I agree with Prof. Yu-tin Huang, my undergraduate mentor and the advisor of this project, that at the beginning we simply want to explore what constraints the physical assumptions of the UV theory impose on the EFT. This is exploratory in nature, and it matches what I originally wanted to do in this project: to study how adding new conditions affects previously established results. For example, one conclusion from the null constraints derived from crossing symmetry (more precisely, permutation symmetry for identical scalar scattering) is that if the UV spectrum allows a heavy spin-2 state, it must also contain states of higher spin (spin > 2). The next step for the bootstrap program is to look for physical interpretation, or to rediscover, from the standpoint of full self-consistency, the models that saturate the bounds (such as string amplitudes), for example by searching for linear Regge trajectories or for bounds on other physical observables.

Bootstrap Sampling

The mixed-channel null constraints contain kernels with non-polynomial dependence on the spectral mass $M^2$. In our numerical bootstrap, we impose positivity of the functional on a discrete set of spins and masses. We use a base mass grid for each sampled spin, add mass points to resolve large-spin oscillations, and impose separate constraints in the fixed-impact-parameter limit.

Finite Grid

The mass variable is $M^2 = \frac{ m_{\rm{gap}}^2 }{1-x}$ for $x \in [0, 1]$. We use Chebyshev nodes$$ \phi = \frac\pi2+\frac\pi2 \cos(\frac{2k+1}{2}\frac\pi{n_{\rm pts}})\,, $$where $n_{\rm pts} = 150$ is the number of the sampling points in this finite grid and $k = 0, 1, \cdots, n_{\rm pts}-1$.
The angle $\phi$ is related to $x$ by$$ x = \sin^2(\phi/2)\,, $$such that for each sector, we use its continuum threshold $m_{\rm gap}^2$ and map $0\leq x<1$ to $M^2=m_{\rm gap}^2/(1-x)$. The threshold corresponds to $\phi=0$, and $M^2\to\infty$ as $\phi\to\pi$. The 150 Chebyshev nodes lie strictly inside $0<\phi<\pi$ and cluster toward both endpoints in the compactified coordinate.

We sample every allowed spin through $J=200$. Above 200, we use the sparse sets $J = 400, 1000, 10000, 50000$, retaining both parities in the charged sector.

Supplementary Grid

At large spin, the angular factors in the mixed-channel kernels can oscillate rapidly as the spectral mass varies. Let the subscripts N and C denote the neutral and charged sectors, respectively. The angles entering their Gegenbauer polynomials are

$$ \theta_N(M) = \arcsin\!\left(\frac{2m_A}{M}\right), \qquad \theta_C(M) = \arccos\!\left( 1-\frac{2m_A^2(M^2+m_A^2)}{(M^2-m_A^2)^2} \right). $$

At large degree and fixed angle away from the endpoints, the leading Gegenbauer phase has slope $J+\frac{D-3}{2}$

with respect to the angle. A fixed angular interval therefore contains more oscillations as the spin increases. This motivates choosing supplementary points at equal increments of a phase coordinate. In the present calculation we work in ten spacetime dimensions and define

$$ \psi_{s,J}(M) = \left(J+\frac{7}{2}\right)\theta_s(M), \qquad s\in\{N,C\}. $$

For each sampled spin with $J\geq 40,$ we construct a separate supplementary mass grid in each sector. Denote the continuum threshold mass in sector $s$ by $M_{\mathrm{gap},s}$. We use the phase step and impact-parameter cutoff $\Delta\psi=\frac{\pi}{4},\qquad b_{\max}=100$ in the units of the calculation. The number of supplementary points is

$$ K_{s,J} = \left\lfloor \frac{ \min\!\left\{ \left(J+\frac{7}{2}\right)\theta_s(M_{\mathrm{gap},s}), \ m_A b_{\max} \right\}} {\Delta\psi} \right\rfloor . $$

The first entry in the minimum is the phase at the sector threshold. It keeps the supplementary masses within the continuum. The second entry sets a dimensionless phase cutoff, motivated by the fixed-impact-parameter limit below. For each integer index we choose

$$ \theta_{s,j} = \frac{j\,\Delta\psi}{J+7/2}, \qquad j=1,\ldots,K_{s,J}. $$

We then invert the sector angle to obtain the squared mass. Explicitly, with $c_j=1-\cos\theta_{C,j}$, the two inverse maps are

$$ M_{N,j}^2 =\frac{4m_A^2}{\sin^2\theta_{N,j}}, \qquad M_{C,j}^2 =m_A^2\frac{1+c_j+\sqrt{1+4c_j}}{c_j}. $$

These masses are added to the base grid for that sector and spin. The index starts at one because zero angle corresponds to infinite mass.

Impact Parameter Grid

The supplementary grid evaluates kernels at finite mass and spin. We also impose positivity on the limiting kernels obtained by taking the mass and spin to infinity together at fixed impact parameter, $b=\frac{2J}{M}.$
Both sector angles have the same leading behavior at large mass,

$$ \theta_s(M)=\frac{2m_A}{M} +O\!\left(\frac{m_A^3}{M^3}\right), \qquad s\in\{N,C\}. $$

It follows that the phase coordinate used above has a finite limit,

$$ \left(J+\frac{7}{2}\right)\theta_s(M) \longrightarrow m_A b. $$

To take the limit of the angular factor, we use the normalized Gegenbauer polynomial

$$ \mathcal P_J(u) =\frac{C_J^{(\lambda)}(u)}{C_J^{(\lambda)}(1)}, \qquad \lambda=\frac{D-3}{2}. $$

The degree tends to infinity while the argument approaches one. Writing $z=m_A b,\qquad\nu=\lambda-\frac12=\frac{D-4}{2},$

the Mehler–Heine formula gives

$$ \lim_{\substack{J,M\to\infty\\2J/M=b}} \mathcal P_J\!\left(\cos\theta_s(M)\right) = \Gamma(\nu+1)\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^\nu J_\nu(z). $$

In $D = 10$ this reduces to $\frac{48}{z^3}J_3(z).$

The value at zero impact parameter is defined by continuity. Since $J_3(z)\sim z^3/48$ as $z\to0$, the limiting angular factor equals one there, in agreement with the normalization $\mathcal P_J(1)=1$.

We evaluate the limiting positivity blocks at the impact parameters

$$ \mathcal B = \left\{ \frac{\ell\,\Delta\psi}{m_A} \ \middle|\ \ell=0,1,\ldots, \left\lfloor\frac{m_A b_{\max}}{\Delta\psi}\right\rfloor \right\} \cup\{b_{\max}\}. $$

The spacing corresponds to one phase step in the variable $m_A b$. We include both zero and the upper endpoint, even when the last interval is shorter than a full step.

For the mixed null constraints $\chi_{kq}$ used here, the oscillatory term survives in the fixed-impact-parameter limit only for $q=0$. Its angular factor is

$$ {}_0F_1\!\left(;4+k;-\frac{z^2}{4}\right) = \Gamma(4+k) \left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{3+k} J_{3+k}(z). $$

Thus the oscillatory term of the $q=0$ row involves a Bessel function of order $3+k$. Its full coefficient also includes the prefactor specific to that row.
